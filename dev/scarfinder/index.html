ScarFinder

ScarFinder searches for low-entanglement, weakly thermalizing trajectories directly on a variational manifold. One iteration leaves the manifold and projects back onto it:

evolve!(ψ, U, nstep; maxdim)        # evolve off the manifold
r = project!(ψ, χ; terms, target)   # back to bond dimension χ at energy density target

scarfinder_step!(ψ, U, χ; …) runs these two stages, and scarfinder!(ψ, U, χ, N; …) runs N of them. U is anything evolve! accepts:

UExample
a sequence of (G, i, j) gates (matrices, EndpointGates, ControlledGates)trotter_gates(layers, dt; trotter=:second)
a vector of layer MPOstrotter_mpos(layers, dt; n=2)
a single local gate G, applied at every translation of the cellexp(-1im * dt * h), a Floquet or projected step P * U

For the iMPS manifold, project! is truncate! (every bond keeps its χ largest Schmidt values of the canonical form) followed, when target is given, by energy_fix! (normalized imaginary-time steps toward target). It returns (loss, energy_error, converged):

  • loss = −log F_site between the evolved and truncated states, accounting for their norms and measured before energy correction;
  • energy_error = E(ψ) − target after the correction (nothing without target);
  • converged: whether the correction met tol (true without target).

scarfinder_step! uses the bond-dimension projection for dense iMPS and checks the projection arguments before evolution. For a nonempty built-in MPO evolution it can reuse the normalized Schmidt basis when the cut is well separated and the transfer has an isolated dominant fixed point. After slicing, the gauge is restored on the retained support. This can change the tensor gauge and roundoff compared with separate calls to evolve! and project!. Gate sequences are checked as each gate is applied, so a later error can leave earlier updates in place. For a different variational manifold, compose your evolution and projection in an explicit loop.

For an analytic iMPS ansatz, construct the starting state with iMPS(T, tensors(q)) and evolve it using the same evolve! API. To fit the endpoint, pass the candidate's raw tensors and the evolved right-weighted tensors ψ.Γ to mixed_logoverlap. The fit can use the analytic tensor derivatives without canonicalizing every trial. If the candidate norm varies with q, include the candidate log norm and its derivative in the objective. The chart, constraints and optimizer belong to the fit.

The energy

The energy is given by terms, a list of (h, i, j) (sites i ≤ j of the chain, repeated with period n) or of commuting layers of them, as for energy_fix!. The period n defaults to the cell length of the first MPO, or ψ.n. Two shortcuts take a local density h, which stands for (h, i, i + span − 1) on every site of the cell:

scarfinder!(ψ, U, h, χ, N; target)               # any U, energy from h
scarfinder!(ψ, h, dt, χ, N; target, trotter)      # U built from h and dt as well

In the (h, dt) form, trotter=nothing applies exp(−i dt h) at every translation of the cell, left to right; trotter=:second, :fourth or :fourth_opt uses trotter_gates(uniform_layers(ψ, h), dt; trotter), and layers=… replaces the automatic grouping by a commuting decomposition of your own.

For constrained models the dynamics and the energy differ: the gates carry the projector, the energy is the bare Hamiltonian. Pass both:

U = trotter_mpos(layers, dt; n=2, gate_map=G -> P3 * G)   # every gate is P3 * U
scarfinder!(ψ, U, 2, N; terms=layers, target=e, n=2, nstep=20, maxdim=16, refine=false)

The correction uses ordinary imaginary-time gates built from terms. The next real-time window projects out the small leakage introduced by it; measure it with constraint_leakage(ψ, Blockade()).

Keyword arguments

  • nstep=10: microscopic steps per iteration. One iteration covers physical time Δt ≈ nstep · dt; nstep = 1 warns, since one step followed by a hard projection is usually too aggressive.
  • maxdim=MAXDIM: bond dimension the evolution may grow into before the projection to χ; typically larger than χ.
  • cutoff=SVDTOL: singular-value cutoff of the evolution and the projection.
  • orth_tol=nothing sets the orthonormalization tolerance for MPO evolution, Schmidt truncation and imaginary-time energy correction. See truncate!.
  • terms, target=nothing, tol=1e-6, alpha=0.1, maxstep=50, n: energy correction, see energy_fix!.
  • refine=true, refine_step=100, refine_U=U (refine_dt=dt/10 in the (h, dt) form): after the N iterations, scan refine_step further steps with refine_U and keep the state of lowest maximal bond entropy. Set refine=false to run exactly N iterations.
  • history: a vector that collects the N results.
  • observe: a function f(ψ, k, r), called with k = 0 and r = nothing on the initial state and after each iteration with the candidate and its result. ψ is the live state; copy it to keep it. Refinement samples are neither observed nor in history.

Composing the loop yourself

gates = trotter_gates(uniform_layers(ψ, h), dt; trotter=:fourth)
terms = [(h, i, i + 2) for i in 1:ψ.n]
for _ in 1:N
    evolve!(ψ, gates, nstep; maxdim)
    r = project!(ψ, χ; terms, target)
    r.converged || break
end

minimize_on_trajectory! is the refinement scan with your own step and objective:

advance!(state) = evolve!(state, gates, 1; maxdim)
objective(state) = maximum(i -> ent_S(state, i), 1:state.n)
selection = minimize_on_trajectory!(objective, advance!, ψ, 50)

It considers ψ and the states after each of the 50 steps, writes the first minimizer into ψ and returns selection.step and selection.value.

Recording a release

evolve!(ψ, mpos, steps; observe=f, every=k) calls f(φ, s, stats) at s = 0, every k complete Trotter steps, and at the last step. φ is the state at time s·dt, identical to evolve!(copy, mpos, s) from the same input; at a merged half-step boundary the pending half step is applied to a snapshot, and the main trajectory is unchanged. Splitting a window into single-step calls instead changes the integrator: at χ = 2, ten calls of evolve!(…, 1) differed from one ten-step window by 1.36e-5 in n_A.

release_recorder records physical time, bond entropies, retained dimensions, discarded weight, num_saturated (requested dimension exceeded the cap), num_at_cap (retained dimension equal to the cap), site occupations and the per-site return fidelity. How to turn these into a score is up to the caller.

A complete PXP example

The gate G contains a two-site blockade projector that restores the Rydberg-constraint subspace after each truncation; the energy target is that of the initial Z2 product state.

using InfiniteTEBD
using LinearAlgebra

P0 = [0 0; 0 1]
N1 = [1 0; 0 0]
X = [0 1; 1 0]
h_pxp = kron(P0, X, P0)                                  # three-site PXP density
no_double_2 = Matrix{Float64}(I, 4, 4) - kron(N1, N1)    # two-site blockade
dt = 0.01
G = kron(no_double_2, I(2)) * kron(I(2), no_double_2) * exp(-1im * dt * h_pxp)

psi = product_iMPS(ComplexF64, [[0, 1], [1, 0], [0, 1], [1, 0]])
target = energy_density(psi, h_pxp)

# One ScarFinder iteration represents Δt = 0.05.
scarfinder!(psi, G, h_pxp, 2, 5; nstep=5, target, maxdim=12, refine=false)

(; target_energy=target, final_energy=energy_density(psi, h_pxp),
   maxbond=maximum(length.(psi.λ)))
(target_energy = 0.0, final_energy = 2.933206556510663e-16, maxbond = 2)

A full grid point of the staggered PXP phase diagram, with seeds, recording and a release, is examples/pxp_scarfinder_point.jl.

Notes

  • rand_iMPS(rng, T, n, d, dim) draws from the same uniform distribution as rand_iMPS(T, n, d, dim); derive one RNG per task from (grid point, seed id) for reproducible runs.
  • translate(ψ, k) shifts the site tensors with their spectra. On the staggered chain a one-site translation maps H(μ, δ) to H(μ, −δ).
  • per_site_fidelity returns the amplitude |⟨a|b⟩|^(1/n); a squared per-site overlap is its square.
  • constraint_leakage is the per-site negative log of the legal amplitude, not the maximal double occupancy.
  • energy_span estimates the lowest and highest energy densities reachable by imaginary-time evolution with h, useful for picking target.

Reference

J. Ren, A. Hallam, L. Ying, and Z. Papić, ScarFinder: A Detector of Optimal Scar Trajectories in Quantum Many-Body Dynamics, PRX Quantum 6, 040332 (2025).
